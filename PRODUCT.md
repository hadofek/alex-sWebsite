# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Prospective guitar students at any stage — from someone who just picked up their first electric and doesn't know where to start, to an intermediate player who has hit a ceiling and suspects the problem is not the technique but the thinking, to an advanced player who wants to understand why they play the way they do. They come with curiosity, frustration, and something they want to say with an instrument but can't yet articulate. They are musicians before they are students. They are not buying a service. They are beginning a practice.

Context: someone alone at a desk, late evening, searching with intent. They have likely already rejected a few other options — too corporate, too impersonal, too much like a course. They are looking for a teacher, not a platform.

## Product Purpose

A portal into a specific pedagogical philosophy: that music is a practice of becoming, that technique serves expression, and that the right teacher can collapse years of searching into months of clarity. Alex teaches the instrument as a way of thinking — about sound, about time, about what it means to have a voice.

The site exists to make one thing undeniable: Alex is that teacher for you.

Success is a prospect filling out the contact form — not because a CTA convinced them, but because they read three paragraphs and thought "yes, this is exactly what I've been looking for."

## Brand Personality

Intentional. Searching. Earned.

Not warm-and-approachable in the tutoring-platform sense. Warm the way a rehearsal room is warm — serious, focused, alive. The voice is a musician who has thought deeply about why they play, not just how. Confident without being loud about it.

## Anti-references

- **Fender Play / Guitar Center** — retail-adjacent, product-first, brand over substance
- **JustinGuitar / TrueFire / GuitarTricks** — platform feel, subscription mindset, lessons as content library
- **Generic music school sites** — bright, cheerful, institutional, no personality
- **SaaS landing pages** — hero metrics, emoji icon grids, pricing tables with feature-checklist rows
- **Anything that treats a lesson as a transaction** — "buy now," "plans," "packages" as the primary frame

## Design Principles

1. **The teacher is the work.** Alex's voice and philosophy should be felt in every section. This is not a template. There is one person here, and the site is his argument.

2. **Silence is part of the music.** Negative space is not emptiness — it is where meaning accumulates. The design does not rush the visitor. It lets them arrive.

3. **The student is a musician first.** Speak to their artistic aspiration, not their purchase decision. They don't want to buy lessons. They want to find their sound.

4. **Craft accumulates.** Every design choice should feel deliberate and considered — not assembled from parts. The way a musician's style is the sum of a thousand small decisions made over years.

5. **Depth over breadth.** One compelling idea per section, expressed fully. Not a feature list. Not a grid of icons. A point of view, repeated with variation.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Dark background with warm light text — ensure contrast ratios hold across all text sizes, especially the muted and secondary body copy. Respect prefers-reduced-motion for all entrance animations. Skill level labels (Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced) should never rely on color alone.
